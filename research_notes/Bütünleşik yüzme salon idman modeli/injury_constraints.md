# Injury Prevention & Exercise Prescription Under Health Constraints (50+ Masters Swimmer + Gym)

> **DISCLAIMER:** These are general evidence summaries for app rule design, not medical advice. Most evidence on these topics is low-to-moderate certainty (small cohorts, surveys, biomechanics in healthy subjects, expert consensus). Rules derived from it should be conservative defaults that a clinician can override. Several primary sources (PubMed, PMC, BJSM, Aspetar) were blocked by the network proxy during this research. Where a claim relies on search-index summaries instead of full text, it is marked "(abstract/summary-level)".

Certainty legend: **[H]** high (CPG grade A or consistent RCTs) · **[M]** moderate · **[L]** low (single small study, biomechanics, expert/coaching opinion) · **[BK]** background knowledge, not verified in this session.

## 1. Swimmer's shoulder / subacromial pain / rotator cuff

### Takeaway
Risk is driven mainly by **load changes** (acute:chronic workload spikes, volume), prior pain, and fatigue of the posterior cuff and scapular muscles. Laxity, rotation ROM and scapular findings are only modestly predictive. Prevention programs center on **dry-land open-chain external rotation (ER) strengthening plus scapular retractors and serratus, with pec stretching, for at least 6–8 weeks**. Evidence for actual pain reduction is weak to moderate. Gym exercises that load end-range internal rotation with abduction, or end-range ER/extension, should be modified.

### Cited Findings
- **Risk factors (best prospective data):** In the highest-quality study in a 2023 systematic review (n = 201), a **high acute:chronic workload ratio (ACWR)** and **reduced posterior shoulder strength-endurance** were associated with injury. Across 80 variables, there was "no strong evidence" for any single factor. There was moderate evidence for competitive level and for altered muscle recruitment (e.g., increased serratus anterior activity) [M] — [McKenzie et al. 2023, Scand J Med Sci Sports, doi 10.1111/sms.14454](https://www.citedrive.com/en/discovery/shoulder-pain-and-injury-risk-factors-in-competitive-swimmers-a-systematic-review/)
- In a 2021 AJSM prospective cohort, **ACWR predicted shoulder pain that interferes with training (OR 4.31; 95% CI 1.00–18.54)**. **Hand-entry position error** (technique) was also a predictor. There were 42 new cases and AUC was 0.71, so the estimate is imprecise [L–M] — [summary via IJSPT/related review](https://ijspt.scholasticahq.com/article/90282); [PubMed 33211610](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33211610/)
- A 2015 critical systematic review found **no risk factor with high certainty**. Moderate certainty: clinical joint laxity/instability, IR/ER ROM, **previous history of pain/injury**, and competitive level [M] — [Hill et al. 2015, Phys Sportsmed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26366502/)
- A single swim session acutely reduces shoulder **ER ROM, pectoralis minor length and isometric rotation torque**. Weekly accumulated load reduces ER ROM and worsens wellness (soreness, fatigue, sleep) [L–M] — [IJSPT review](https://ijspt.scholasticahq.com/article/90282)
- **Volume–pain link:** Feijen et al. 2020 (J Athl Train, doi 10.4085/1062-6050-439-18) reviewed swim-training volume vs shoulder pain across 4 life-span age groups. A summary reports **moderate associations between volume and shoulder pain in adolescents**. I could not retrieve its masters-age findings [M for adolescents; masters unknown] — [Feijen 2020 JAT PDF](https://nata.kglmeridian.com/downloadpdf/view/journals/attr/55/1/article-p32.pdf); [paddles/volume search summary](https://ijspt.scholasticahq.com/article/72717.pdf)
- **Mechanism / pain phase:** Pain typically appears in **pull-through, when the arm is maximally internally rotated and adducted** [L] — [Epistemonikos-indexed review](https://www.epistemonikos.org/es/documents/8638e0c54c57768f799c56b7a8f97ae213a3cf9b)
- **Scapular / posterior structures:** A short pectoralis minor is linked to increased scapular internal rotation and anterior tilt. Posterior shoulder stiffness (reduced passive GH IR) is linked to greater anterior tilt. Both are proposed risk factors for chronic shoulder pain in overhead athletes [L–M] — [Cools et al. 2013, scapular dyskinesis rehab](https://oliverfinlay.com/assets/pdf/cools%20et%20al%20(2013)%20rehabilitation%20of%20scapular%20dyskinesis%20-%20from%20the%20office%20worker%20to%20the%20elite%20overhead%20athlete.pdf)
- In 50 adult elite swimmers, more severe scapular dyskinesis was associated with **shorter pec minor and reduced IR ROM**. It was **not related to shoulder pain** but was related to lower Penn Shoulder Scores [L] — [2025 cross-sectional study, PMC12565977](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12565977/)
- Struyf et al. 2017 (BJSM 51:775) is a clinical review of swimmer's shoulder impairments: muscle activity, strength, endurance, motor control, ROM, laxity/instability, posture, and scapular dyskinesis. I obtained only the abstract — [BJSM](https://bjsm.bmj.com/content/51/10/775)
- **Prevention programs:** A systematic review of 14 studies reported the following [L–M] — [systematic review, 2021 (PubMed 33409808)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33409808/); [Epistemonikos record](https://www.epistemonikos.org/es/documents/8638e0c54c57768f799c56b7a8f97ae213a3cf9b)
  - A **6–8 week program combining ER and scapular retractor strengthening with pectoral stretching** may lower shoulder pain incidence.
  - Strengthening for **more than 12 weeks** improved ER peak force, endurance and the ER:IR ratio, but did not clearly reduce pain.
  - **Open-chain dry-land** exercises beat closed-chain and in-water training for ER strength and endurance.
- A registered RCT (NCT06552585, 30 competitive swimmers, 12 weeks) compares weights and elastic bands against sham. It uses ER at 90° abduction and prone **Y's and T's**. Results are not confirmed [registry] — [ClinicalTrials.gov](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT06552585)
- A collegiate corrective program reported that participants were **44% less likely to sustain upper-extremity injury** [L, thesis] — [UNR thesis](https://scholarwolf.unr.edu/entities/thesis/8c651df6-8db9-4856-a551-4694d1d7fdd2)
- **Timing:** A USA Swimming-linked series advises doing cuff and scapular exercises **after practice, not before**, to avoid pre-fatiguing stabilisers [L, expert] — [Shoulder injury prevention PDF](https://www.gomotionapp.com/arlrad/UserFiles/File/Shoulder%20injury%20prevention.pdf)
- **Paddles / technique:** The Vanderbilt clinical handout lists **hand paddles** among factors to address, along with anterior capsule stretching, single-side breathing and dropped-elbow drills. No controlled study of paddles vs shoulder pain was found [L] — [VUMC swimming shoulder handout](https://www.vumc.org/sports-medicine/sites/vumc.org.sports-medicine/files/public_files/documents/swimmingshoulder.pdf)
- **Gym exercises:** The evidence here is coaching-level mechanism reasoning only [L]:
  - **Upright row:** Elevating the elbows above shoulder height with the humerus internally rotated (narrow grip) approximates the greater tuberosity to the acromion. Suggested modifications: grip at least hip width, retract the scapulae, pull only to the lower ribs, elbows no higher than shoulder level. Age-related acromial spurs may increase risk — [Les Mills](https://www.lesmills.com/fit-planet/fitness/upright-rows)
  - **Behind-neck press and pulldown:** These load the shoulder in end-range ER/abduction, "not a position you want to load excessively" — [exercises to avoid (swim club PT page)](https://www.gomotionapp.com/team/fgcspst/page/news/77827/exercises-to-avoid); [Train smarter at the gym](https://bpbpd.wpengine.com/sports/train-smarter-at-the-gym-part-2)
  - **Dips:** No source was found in this session. The common clinical view is that deep dips (shoulder extension beyond neutral with anterior translation) stress the anterior capsule [BK].

### Inferences (candidate app rules)
- **R-SH1 Load cap:** Flag when the weekly swim distance ACWR is above about 1.3 (warn) or above about 1.5 (block or suggest a deload). Cap week-to-week volume increases at about 10%. The ACWR is the best-supported modifiable factor, but the exact thresholds are conventional, not swim-validated.
- **R-SH2 Paddles:** Require a pain-free baseline (MSI = 0 for the previous 7 days). Limit paddles to at most 10–15% of session volume, progress gradually, start with small paddles, use them early in the session (not when fatigued), and stop at any shoulder pain. Usage rules are inferred from load and fatigue logic, not from trials.
- **R-SH3 Prevention block:** 2–3 times per week after swimming or in the gym session. Include:
  - side-lying or band **ER** (0° and 90° abduction)
  - prone **Y/T/W**, rows (scapular retractors)
  - serratus work (push-up plus, wall slide)
  - pec minor doorway stretch plus posterior shoulder (sleeper/cross-body) stretch when IR is limited

  Dose: 2–3 sets of 10–15 (endurance bias), progress at 6–8 weeks, and continue for at least 12 weeks.
- **R-SH4 Gym substitutions:** Replace the upright row with a high pull to chest level or with lateral raises in the scapular plane, below 90°, thumbs up. Ban behind-neck press and pulldown and use front variants. Limit dips to shoulder extension near neutral or replace them with close-grip push-ups or the bench press. Overhead press is allowed only if MSI = 0. Otherwise use a landmine or incline press.
- **R-SH5 Pull buoy:** It reduces kick contribution and so increases arm load per metre. Count pull sets toward the upper-limb load in the same way as paddles (inferred).
- **R-SH6 Fatigue:** Avoid hard pull or paddle sets after heavy upper-body gym days. Leave about 24–48 h between heavy pressing or pulling gym work and high-intensity pull sets (inferred from acute post-session torque and ROM losses).

### Gaps
- No RCT directly tests paddle or pull-buoy restrictions. Masters-specific (50+) shoulder pain epidemiology was not retrieved. The masters age-band results of Feijen 2020 were not extracted. The full text of Wanivenhaus 2012 (Sports Health) was blocked; its recommended stroke-mechanics specifics (breathing bilaterally, body roll, avoiding a crossover entry) are known [BK] but not verified here.

## 2. Adult sequelae of Legg-Calvé-Perthes disease (LCPD)

### Takeaway
A healed LCPD hip often has an **aspherical, enlarged femoral head (coxa magna/plana), a short neck, high trochanter and sometimes acetabular retroversion**. This produces FAI-like (cam/"pincer" or extra-articular) impingement and a raised risk of early OA, especially with worse Stulberg and lateral-pillar grades. Exercise guidance has to be extrapolated from FAI syndrome care. That care is exercise-first, strengthens the hip abductors and core, and modifies deep flexion with IR/adduction. Low-impact, well-controlled loading is preferred.

### Cited Findings
- Residual deformities persist into adulthood. Their severity relates to epiphyseal involvement, age at onset, lateral acetabular deficiency, leg-length discrepancy and treatment. They can cause anterior impingement, and joint incongruence predisposes to early OA. Patients with **lateral pillar B, B/C or C** frequently had pain and radiographic OA. **Clinical FAI signs were associated with pain and lower function scores.** **Stulberg III–IV** hips more often had poor or fair outcomes [M, observational] — [Acta Orthop Belg, Hip deformity in symptomatic adult Perthes](https://www.actaorthopaedica.be/archive/volume-72/issue-6/original-studies/hip-deformity-in-symptomatic-adult-perthes-disease/); [EM-Consulte FAI in LCPD](https://www.em-consulte.com/it/article/926701/-femoroacetabular-impingement-legg-calve-perthes-d); [AAOS 2023 abstract](https://index.mirasmart.com/aaos2023/PDFfiles/AAOS2023-010157.PDF)
- In young adults after childhood LCPD (15 hips), the femoral head showed anterior bulging averaging **115% of the functional radius**, and acetabular retroversion was present in at least one third of hips. This favors anterior impingement [L] — [Acta Orthop Belg](https://www.actaorthopaedica.be/archive/volume-72/issue-6/original-studies/hip-deformity-in-symptomatic-adult-perthes-disease/)
- Hip arthroscopy can address osseous and chondral abnormalities in the healed LCPD hip, and monitoring after skeletal maturity for FAI is suggested [L] — [PMC3031125 (index)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3031125); [raaot](https://raaot.org.ar/index.php/AAOTMAG/article/download/1709/5467)
- **FAI syndrome exercise evidence (extrapolated):**
  - The international consensus says first-line treatment should be **exercise-based and last at least 3 months**, but no specific evidence-based protocol has been established [M] — [British Hip Society / UK Hip Physio Network, 2024](https://britishhipsociety.com/Portals/0/Downloads/UK%20Hip%20Physiotherapy%20Network/PHYSIOTHERAPY-AS-CONSERVATIVE-TREATMENT-FOR-FAI-SYNDROME-13.12.2024.pdf)
  - **UK FASHIoN RCT:** Arthroscopy beat Personalised Hip Therapy by 6.8 iHOT-33 points (MCID 6.1). However, the physiotherapy-led group still improved from 35.6 to 49.7 [H for the comparison] — [UK FASHIoN summary / Lancet 2018](https://boneandjoint.org.uk/Article/10.1302/1358-992X.2018.9.004); [Keele repository](https://eprints.keele.ac.uk/4979/)
- **Activities to modify in FAI** [L–M, guidance]:
  - prolonged sitting, especially **cross-legged sitting (flexion + IR + adduction)**
  - **deep squatting into full hip flexion**
  - racing-cycling position

  Suggested alternatives are a quad-dominant squat (more upright trunk) and a wider stance. Strengthen the gluteus medius and lumbopelvic stabilisers — [British Hip Society guidance](https://britishhipsociety.com/Portals/0/Downloads/UK%20Hip%20Physiotherapy%20Network/PHYSIOTHERAPY-AS-CONSERVATIVE-TREATMENT-FOR-FAI-SYNDROME-13.12.2024.pdf); [Beacon Ortho](https://www.beaconortho.com/blog/exercising-fai/)
- **Breaststroke kick and hip:** Pain was associated with **less hip IR**, more breaststroke volume and less warm-up. Injury was more likely when hip abduction at kick initiation was **below 37° or above 42°** (n = 21 subgroup). Some 42% of breaststrokers reportedly missed training because of groin injury (secondary source) [L] — [breaststroke search summary, yourswimlog citing Rovere & Nichols / Vizsolyi](https://www.yourswimlog.com/how-to-fix-and-prevent-breaststrokers-knee); [Aspetar: knee and back in swimming](https://www.aspetar.com/Journal/viewarticle.aspx?id=263)

### Inferences (candidate app rules)
- **R-HIP1 Loaded hip-flexion cap:** For a "Perthes/FAI" constraint, cap loaded hip flexion at about 90°. Use box squats to parallel or higher, trap-bar deadlifts from blocks, and step-ups to a knee-height box. Avoid deep or ATG squats, deep leg press (sled close to the chest), and loaded flexion combined with adduction and IR (e.g., a narrow-stance deep goblet squat, or the rear-foot elevated split squat with knee-in valgus).
- **R-HIP2 Preferred exercises:**
  - hip abductors (side-lying abduction, banded lateral walk, side plank with abduction)
  - extensors (glute bridge, hip thrust, cable kickback)
  - isometric adductor squeezes
  - core anti-rotation
  - single-leg balance

  Keep the hip in neutral or mild flexion.
- **R-HIP3 Impact:** Prefer low impact (swim, cycle with a raised saddle and handlebar, elliptical, walking) over running and jumping. Plyometrics are allowed only at low volume and only if the hip is asymptomatic (inferred from OA risk; the bone-loading benefit needs to be balanced, see §5).
- **R-HIP4 Breaststroke kick:** Default to limiting breaststroke kick volume, e.g., at most 20–25% of session kick distance and no kick-only breaststroke sets when hip or knee MSI is 1 or higher. Prefer a narrower kick within the 37–42° abduction band, with a thorough warm-up. Substitute flutter or dolphin kick, or pull with a buoy (minding shoulder load). Thresholds are inferred.
- **R-HIP5 Mobility:** Do gentle hip ROM within the pain-free range. Avoid forceful end-range flexion/IR stretching into impingement (e.g., the aggressive pigeon stretch can compress the anterior hip). Prefer hip flexor and quad stretching (extension side).

### Gaps
- No studies specifically address exercise prescription for adult post-LCPD hips. All guidance is extrapolated from FAI syndrome and hip OA. There are no quantified OA risk figures for a typical untreated adult LCPD hip. The final results of the 95-hip cohort were not retrieved. No data was found on breaststroke in LCPD hips.

## 3. Patellofemoral pain (PFP) / chondromalacia patellae

### Takeaway
**Combined hip- plus knee-targeted exercise therapy is the core treatment** (JOSPT CPG 2019 and international consensus 2016/2018), effective short-, medium- and long-term [H]. Passive modalities alone are not recommended. Patellofemoral joint (PFJ) stress depends on the knee angle: closed-chain (squat) stress is lowest at 0–45°, and open-chain (knee extension) stress is lowest at 90–45°. This supports angle-limited exercise selection with progression guided by pain.

### Cited Findings
- **Willy et al. 2019** JOSPT CPG (49(9):CPG1–95, doi 10.2519/jospt.2019.0302) [H] — [GWU repository](https://hsrc.himmelfarb.gwu.edu/smhs_hs_facpubs/48); [LER Magazine](https://lermagazine.com/?p=33487); [Consultant360](https://www.consultant360.com/exclusive/rheumatology/orthopedics/academy-orthopaedic-physical-therapy-develops-guideline)
  - Clinicians **should** use combined hip- and knee-targeted exercise to reduce pain and improve function in the short, medium and long term.
  - Biophysical agents should not be used: ultrasound, cryotherapy, phonophoresis, iontophoresis, electrical stimulation, laser.
  - Mobilization and manipulation should not be used stand-alone.
  - Diagnosis relies on reproducing pain on squatting.
  - Outcome measures: AKPS, KOOS-PF, VAS-activity.
  - **Prevention:** gradually increase activity volume. Adolescents who specialise in one sport have 28% higher PFP risk.
- **Collins et al. 2018** consensus (BJSM 52:1170–78; 41 experts, 5th International PFP Retreat, updating the Manchester 2016 statement) supports exercise therapy, **especially combined hip + knee** [H] — [BJSM abstract](https://bjsm.bmj.com/content/52/18/1170.abstract); [EUR repository](https://pure.eur.nl/en/publications/2018-consensus-statement-on-exercise-therapy-and-physical-interve/)
  - Full recommendations on orthoses, taping and manual therapy were not retrieved. [BK]: the consensus also recommends foot orthoses (short term), patellar taping (short term), and combining with education; knee mobilisations alone and lumbar mobilisation are not recommended.
- **PFJ stress by angle:** Powers et al. 2014 (n = 10 healthy) [L–M, biomechanics] — [Physiotutors summary](https://physiotutors.com/leg-extension-dangerous-for-your-knees-or-great-rehab-exercise/); [USUHS record](https://scholar.usuhs.edu/en/publications/patellofemoral-joint-stress-during-weight-bearing-and-non-weight-/)
  - **Squat** produced higher PFJ stress at **90°, 75° and 60°** of knee flexion.
  - **Non-weight-bearing knee extension** produced higher stress at **30°, 15° and 0°**.
  - Recommendation: **squat 45°→0°, knee extension 90°→45°**.
- **Conflicting evidence on squat depth:** One review reports that squat and lunge forces exceed open-chain forces beyond 60°, but only one clinical study included squats deeper than 60°. A 2001 study found **squatting 70°→110° changed PFJ kinetics little**. Clinically, open-chain and closed-chain exercise were equally effective in the only comparative trial [L] — [PubMed 11390050](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11390050/); [PMC4901792 (index)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4901792)
- **Breaststroke knee:** in surveys [L–M]:
  - Knee pain was reported by **73% of breaststroke specialists vs 48% of other swimmers** (n = 391). Age, years of competitive swimming and training characteristics correlated with knee pain.
  - In 36 breaststrokers, 75% had at least 3 episodes per season and 47% had weekly episodes.
  - Pain is typically **medial** (MCL/medial plica), from high **valgus** load as the knee extends rapidly with tibial external rotation. This is a valgus-medial mechanism rather than a primarily patellofemoral one.

  Sources — [McMaster "Breaststroker's knee"](https://experts.mcmaster.ca/scholarly-works/852812); [Aspetar](https://www.aspetar.com/Journal/viewarticle.aspx?id=263)
- **Masters study:** There was no significant relationship between master swimmers' age and pain, diagnosis or surgical history (fragment only) [L] — [PMC7336593](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7336593)

### Inferences (candidate app rules)
- **R-PF1 Exercise mix:** Each lower-body session for a PFP-constrained user includes at least one hip exercise (abduction, external rotation, extension) and at least one knee/quad exercise.
- **R-PF2 Angle windows** (default while knee MSI ≥ 1; relax when MSI = 0 for 2 weeks):
  - Closed-chain (squat, leg press, split squat): **0–45° knee flexion** (shallow box squat, partial leg press).
  - Open-chain (leg extension): **90–45°** only; avoid terminal extension under load.
  - Progress depth by about 10–15° only after 2 consecutive pain-free (MSI 0–1) sessions.
- **R-PF3 Avoid while symptomatic:** deep loaded lunges or squats past 90°, jumping or landing volume, full-range leg extension with heavy load, and prolonged kneeling.
- **R-PF4 Swim:** If knee MSI ≥ 1, limit breaststroke kick (same caps as R-HIP4). Flutter kick with a pull buoy is the fallback. Watch fins volume (larger knee flexion and extension moments) [inferred]. Medial knee pain specifically points to breaststroke kick.
- **R-PF5 Volume progression:** Increase lower-limb load gradually (CPG prevention statement), e.g., at most about 10% per week in sets × reps × load.

### Gaps
- No direct evidence for chondromalacia (cartilage lesion) vs PFP-specific prescriptions. Rules extrapolate from PFP and knee OA. The complete Collins 2018 recommendation list and grades were not retrieved. There is no data on 50+ masters breaststrokers' knee pain prevalence.

## 4. Pain-monitoring model and traffic-light load rules

### Takeaway
RCT evidence in Achilles tendinopathy (Silbernagel 2007) shows that **continuing activity within a pain-monitoring model gives outcomes no worse than rest**. The model derives from Thomeé's PFP model (1990s). There is a recurring discrepancy about whether the 5/10 limit is "≤5" or "<5". A conservative implementation treats **pain above 5/10 during activity, or pain and stiffness that do not settle by the next morning, or that worsen week to week** as a "reduce load" signal.

### Cited Findings
- **Silbernagel et al. 2007** RCT (AJSM, n = 38) [M] — [physicaltherapyfirst review](https://physicaltherapyfirst.com/blog/continued-sports-activity-using-a-pain-monitoring-model-during-rehabilitation-in-patients-with-achilles-tendinopathy/); [PEDro 18389](https://search.pedro.org.au/search-results/record-detail/18389); [Silbernagel thesis](https://gupea.ub.gu.se/handle/2077/504?show=full)
  - Design: continued running and jumping under the pain-monitoring model vs **active rest** (no exercise for 6 weeks). Both groups did the same progressive tendon-loading program (12 weeks to 6 months).
  - Result: continued activity was **not detrimental**.
- **Threshold wording conflict** [L, secondary sources] — [Physio Field Guide](https://www.patreon.com/physiofieldguide/posts/midportion-75112715); [physicaltherapyfirst](https://physicaltherapyfirst.com/?p=10787)
  - One clinical summary: activity allowed **up to 5/10**. Back off if pain does not settle after exercise, persists next morning, or pain and stiffness increase the following day.
  - Another review of the 2001/2007 guidelines: pain was **not allowed to reach 5** on the 0–10 VAS.
  - The protocol introduces the model in weeks 1–2 (JOSPT 2015 doi 10.2519/jospt.2015.5885).
- **Thomeé model (PFP):** The 1995 Gothenburg thesis "Patellofemoral pain syndrome in young women ... with a model for treatment". Companion work (40 women with PFPS) found pain associated with activity level and suggested **chronic overload** rather than malalignment as the driver [L–M] — [GUPEA thesis](https://gupea.ub.gu.se/items/a9f615ab-84cf-422c-9a51-076b9b39c1cc); [BISp record](https://www.bisp-surf.de/Record/PU199509103060)
  - [BK, unverified] Thomeé 1997 (Scand J Med Sci Sports, "PFPS in young women II. Treatment using a clinical model") allowed exercise with limited pain that settled by the next day.
- **Exercise that is deliberately painful** in PFP has been explored (2015 case report). Evidence is very low — [Nottingham repository](https://nottingham-repository.worktribe.com/OutputFile/783137)

### Inferences (candidate app rules — mapping MSI 0–3 to actions)
The MSI 0–3 scale is the app's own. This mapping is a proposed translation, not validated.

| MSI | Approx. NRS 0–10 | Meaning | Action |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | 0 | No pain | **Green.** Progress per plan (≤10%/wk volume, depth/angle progression allowed). |
| 1 | 1–3 | Mild, "acceptable" | **Green-amber.** Continue at the same load and do not progress that region. Pain must settle within 24 h (next-morning check). |
| 2 | 4–5 | Moderate, at the model ceiling | **Amber.** Keep the session but cut affected-region load or volume by about 30–50%. Substitute exercises (angle windows, no paddles or breaststroke kick). No progression for 1 week. |
| 3 | ≥6 | Exceeds model limit | **Red.** Stop the provoking exercise or stroke. Rest that region 48–72 h (other regions OK). Two red days in 7, or pain at rest or at night → recommend a clinician. |

Additional triggers:
- If next-morning pain or stiffness is higher than the pre-session baseline, downgrade the next session one colour.
- If the weekly trend is worsening across 2 consecutive weeks, apply a deload week.
- Treat any swelling, locking, giving way, night pain or neurological symptoms as red flags: stop and refer.

### Gaps
- The exact original wording of Silbernagel's threshold (≤5 vs <5) was not verified from primary text (PubMed blocked). Evidence for pain-monitoring in **joint** conditions (PFP, hip OA, FAI) is weaker than in tendinopathy. Thomeé's original thresholds were not retrieved. No validation exists for any 4-point (0–3) mapping.

## 5. Age-related considerations (50+ masters swimmer)

### Takeaway
Swimming is non-osteogenic. Swimmers often have **lower BMD than controls** at the legs and total body. A 50+ swimmer therefore needs **heavy, progressive resistance training plus some impact** for bone. LIFTMOR showed that supervised high-intensity training (5×5 at >85% 1RM, twice weekly) is effective and was tolerated even in low-bone-mass postmenopausal women. Tendon and recovery adaptations with age argue for gradual progression and more recovery between hard sessions. These were not directly researched here.

### Cited Findings
- **Swimmer BMD:** Male swimmers had significantly **lower leg aBMD (−9.8%) and total-body aBMD (−7.0%)** vs controls. Endurance swimmers were −14.8% and −10.4%. Runners had **+6.7% leg aBMD** [M, young adults] — [PMC1978462 (index)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1978462); [Bisp: type and intensity of exercise](https://www.bisp-surf.de/Record/PU200711003147)
- Female collegiate swimmers had BMD lower than non-athletes (Mayo study) [L–M] — [HealthDay summary](https://consumer.healthday.com/bone-and-joint-information-4/osteo-arthritis-news-42/some-female-athletes-risking-weaker-bones-609257.html); [MSU](https://msutoday.msu.edu/news/2007/msu-study-finds-certain-college-female-athletes-have-lower-bone-density-may-increase-risk-of-st)
- **LIFTMOR (Watson et al. 2018, JBMR 33:211)** [M–H for this population] — [JBMR](https://academic.oup.com/jbmr/article/33/2/211/7605709); [PEDro 52584](https://search.pedro.org.au/search-results/record-detail/52584)
  - Population: n = 101 postmenopausal women, mean age ~65, T-score < −1.
  - Intervention: 8 months, twice weekly, 30 min, supervised HiRIT (**5×5 >85% 1RM** deadlift/squat/overhead press plus jumping chin-ups with drop landings) vs low-intensity home training.
  - Results: lumbar spine BMD **+2.9% vs −1.2%**; femoral neck **+0.3% vs −1.9%**; cortical thickness and height favoured HiRIT; function improved.
  - Safety follow-up: no vertebral fractures reported, and kyphosis improved (summary-level).
  - Limitations: PEDro 7/10, unblinded, supervised setting.

### Inferences (candidate app rules)
- **R-AGE1 Bone:** At least 2 gym sessions per week including heavy multi-joint lower-body loading, progressing to about 80–85% 1RM sets of ≤5 once technique is established. Within constraints: trap-bar deadlift from blocks, box squat (hip/PF windows apply). Where hip/knee constraints allow, add low-volume impact (e.g., 20–50 low drop jumps or heel drops). Otherwise use brisk weight-bearing walking. Swimming volume does **not** count toward bone loading.
- **R-AGE2 Progression and recovery:**
  - Load progressions at most about 5–10% per week.
  - At least 48 h between heavy sessions for the same muscle group.
  - Deload every 3–5 weeks.
  - Avoid stacking high-intensity swim sets and heavy gym work for the same region on consecutive days.

  These are inferred from general ageing physiology and were not researched in this session.
- **R-AGE3 Overhead heavy loads** (e.g., the LIFTMOR overhead press) conflict with R-SH4. For a user with a shoulder constraint, substitute landmine press or incline press for the bone/strength stimulus.
